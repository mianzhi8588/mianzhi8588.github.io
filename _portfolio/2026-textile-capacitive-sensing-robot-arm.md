## Research question

Can a wearable textile interface turn natural gestures, contact, and deformation into intuitive robot-arm commands? This project investigates capacitive sensing integrated into textiles as an interface between human motion and robot response.

## Main research

The work connects wearable sensing with real-time acquisition, calibration, and control mapping. The main evaluation concerns are repeatability, response latency, and how consistently an intended human input produces the corresponding robot action.

## My contributions

- Developing textile-integrated capacitive sensing elements for gesture and deformation input.
- Building the acquisition and calibration workflow that connects sensor responses to robot commands.
- Investigating repeatable signal interpretation and intuitive human-robot interaction.

## Current progress

The current demonstration shows an early sensing-to-response concept. Systematic validation of repeatability, latency, and robustness remains in progress; the demonstration does not establish performance across users or long-term use.

## Further development

Future work will examine repeatability under different wearing and deformation conditions, reduce sensitivity to baseline changes, and evaluate how easily users can learn and control the interface.
