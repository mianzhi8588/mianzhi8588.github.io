## Research question

Can a robot program complete its task while still behaving unsafely along the way? RoboPhys studies this gap in programs generated by large language models. The central question is how to evaluate the entire physical execution, explain failures, and verify that a correction improves behavior beyond the final task outcome.

## Main research

The project connects three stages: measuring the gap between functional success and physical correctness, examining the mechanisms behind violations, and re-evaluating corrected programs. Controlled manipulation simulations make it possible to compare task outcomes with what happens during contact, transport, grasping, and placement.

## My contributions

- Built a simulation-based workflow for evaluating generated robot-control programs at the behavior level.
- Designed controlled task and scene comparisons that distinguish task completion from physical-safety violations.
- Developed execution-evidence analysis to investigate failure mechanisms and support targeted program revision and revalidation.

## Main findings

**Task completion alone is an incomplete measure of robot-code reliability.** The recorded executions include programs that reach the intended outcome while violating physical constraints along the way. A clean execution in one scene also does not establish robustness to changes in the environment.

**Repair must be checked across the whole execution.** Removing one visible problem can leave a later contact or grasp failure unresolved. The evaluated repairs recover physically correct completion in many inspected cases, while residual failures show that local corrections do not provide a general safety guarantee.

These are findings from the current simulation study. They do not establish reliability on physical robots or across arbitrary tasks.

## Further development

Future directions include broader task and scene coverage, more systematic robustness evaluation, and tighter feedback between execution analysis and program revision. A longer-term direction is to examine which evaluation principles transfer to physical robotic systems.

## Research context

Collaborative research with Dr. Simin Chen, George Mason University. The study is ongoing. This page summarizes the research questions and qualitative findings; detailed evaluation rules, thresholds, and implementation settings are reserved for the manuscript.
