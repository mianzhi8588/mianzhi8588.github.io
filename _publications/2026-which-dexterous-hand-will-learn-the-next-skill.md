**Authors:** Haoyang Wang, Xinmin Fang, Zhengxiong Li, Ying Wang, and Linfeng Tao.

Submitted to **Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS 2027)** · **Under Review**.

## Research question

Two dexterous hands can succeed at the same familiar task while using different patterns of support and finger motion. Can those contact patterns help a designer choose a fixed hand geometry that will learn a new skill more effectively?

## Research overview

This study investigates contact behavior as a design signal for future learning. It compares source-task success with contact-based criteria that capture how fingers release, reposition, and restore contact while the object remains supported. After design selection, hand geometry stays fixed and new controllers learn withheld skills from scratch using their own task rewards.

Preliminary simulation observations on LEAP and Allegro show different palm-contact and finger-switching behavior among successful rotations. Whether these measurements predict learning on new tasks is the central hypothesis being tested.
