Research portfolio

From reliable sensing to embodied intelligence

My research connects signal recovery, mechanical-system diagnosis, multimodal perception, robot learning, and physical control. Across these projects, I focus on a common question: how can an intelligent physical system remain useful, interpretable, and safe when its sensors, models, or operating conditions are imperfect?

Robot-code reliability · ICLR 2027 · Under review

RoboPhys: Physical Correctness of LLM-Generated Robot Code

This project evaluates the gap between task completion and physical correctness in generated robot programs. Execution evidence reveals hidden violations and guides targeted repairs; remaining failures show why corrected programs still require whole-run validation.

Research question: How should functional success and physical safety be evaluated separately for generated robot programs?

Research overview: measuring the outcome–execution gap, explaining failure mechanisms, and revalidating corrections. Includes qualitative findings and contributions.

Signal recovery · Nature Sensors · With editor

Inertial Sensor Overrange Recovery

This project investigates complementary information in multiaxis motion to recover signals during short-term inertial-sensor saturation. Evaluation on simulations, human-motion recordings, and public UAV datasets shows reduced error relative to clipped input while preserving principal signal features.

Submitted to Nature Sensors; currently With Editor. The project page summarizes contributions, qualitative findings, and remaining recovery limitations.

## How I approach a research system
01

Define failure modes

Separate task success from hidden failure: saturation, unsafe motion, poor transfer, unstable grasping, or ambiguous human input.

02

Build observable evidence

Use simulation, controlled experiments, public datasets, and physical prototypes to make system behavior measurable.

03

Design constrained methods

Combine learning with physical structure, geometry, safety checks, signal priors, or interpretable representations.

04

Evaluate the full loop

Report not only accuracy, but robustness, latency, generalization, failure recovery, and limitations.
