## Overview

I served as a science instructor at Beijing Science Center, where I delivered STEM workshops for primary school students and helped organize interactive science activities. The goal was to make scientific reasoning approachable through observation, demonstration, and hands-on participation.

Rather than presenting engineering as a collection of finished facts, I encouraged students to notice a phenomenon, describe what they expected to happen, test an idea, and compare the outcome with their prediction. This structure made room for curiosity while introducing the habits of evidence-based reasoning.

## Role and Contribution

- Delivered hands-on STEM workshops for young students.
- Organized interactive science activities to foster curiosity in mechanical engineering and scientific exploration.
- Practiced communicating engineering concepts to non-specialist audiences.

## Teaching Approach

- Began with familiar objects or visible physical behavior before introducing terminology.
- Broke multi-step mechanisms into small cause-and-effect relationships.
- Used questions to invite predictions instead of immediately giving an answer.
- Treated unexpected outcomes as opportunities to revise an explanation.
- Adjusted language and pacing based on students' responses and prior knowledge.

## Skills Developed

This experience strengthened lesson pacing, audience-aware explanation, facilitation, and real-time adaptation. It also reinforced the value of visual evidence and physical demonstration—skills that transfer directly to presenting experimental robotics and sensing research.

## Reflection

This outreach experience strengthened my ability to explain technical ideas in accessible language and reinforced my interest in using engineering education to broaden students' exposure to science and technology. It also reminded me that effective communication is part of responsible engineering: people need ways to question, interpret, and build confidence in a technical system.
