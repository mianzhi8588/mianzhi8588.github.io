## Overview

I participated in a faculty-led academic exchange program in Singapore, engaging with academic discussions at NUS and NTU on technological innovation systems, AI governance, and social management. The program provided a broader lens for understanding how technical systems move from laboratories into institutions, markets, and public life.

For me, the most valuable connection was between technical capability and the surrounding conditions that shape adoption: governance, standards, organizational incentives, public trust, and the distribution of benefits and risks.

## Themes

- Technological innovation systems
- AI governance
- Social management
- Institutional frameworks for technology adoption

## Questions I Took Forward

- How do governance structures influence the design and deployment of AI-enabled systems?
- What forms of evidence help institutions decide whether an emerging technology is sufficiently reliable?
- How should engineers communicate uncertainty, limitations, and intended use?
- How can innovation systems encourage experimentation without losing sight of safety and social responsibility?

## Relevance to My Research

These questions connect directly to my current interests in reliable embodied intelligence. Robot-code safety, sensor failure recovery, and multimodal control are not only algorithmic problems; they also involve verification, transparency, human expectations, and appropriate boundaries for deployment.

## Reflection

This experience broadened my understanding of how innovation policy, data governance, and institutional environments shape the adoption of emerging technologies in public and private sectors. It also encouraged me to evaluate research systems not only by what they can do, but by how their behavior can be understood, governed, and trusted.
